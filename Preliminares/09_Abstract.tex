  {Abstract}
  {Ecuador's Organic Law on Personal Data Protection (LOPDP) requires controllers to use risk analysis and management methodologies and to assess the impact of high-risk processing; however, Ecuadorian universities lack a specific methodology for their students' data, and the authority's technical regulations are general in scope. The general objective is to design a risk diagnosis and management methodology for the processing of students' personal data in higher education institutions, integrating the SPDP risk management guide with ISO/IEC 27005:2022. The research follows a qualitative approach and is documentary and comparative: using the functional comparative law method, it contrasts the LOPDP with the legal frameworks of the European Union (GDPR), the United States (FERPA), Brazil (LGPD), Colombia (Law 1581 of 2012) and Chile (Law 21,719 of 2024) across thirteen dimensions, in order to identify the minimum elements the methodology must cover, the optional ones, the gaps in the Ecuadorian law and the good practices that can be adopted without legal reform. The regulatory cut-off date is 16 November 2026. The proposed methodology comprises six phases ---regulatory preparation, institutional diagnosis, risk management, impact assessment, breach response and monitoring---, a checklist covering the seventeen items of Article 12, a maturity scale, a table of legal bases by purpose, a prior screening for impact assessments, fifteen sector scenarios and a spreadsheet template with an application guide. It is validated by a Delphi panel of at least five experts, using the expert competence coefficient and Aiken's V. It is not applied in a real institution. At the cut-off date, the template and the specification of the methodology have been verified; the comparative synthesis and the validation will be completed between December 2026 and February 2027.}
