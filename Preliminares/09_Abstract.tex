  {Abstract}
  {Ecuador's Organic Law on Personal Data Protection (LOPDP) grants data subjects the right to be informed, requires free and informed consent, and obliges controllers to manage processing risks; however, there is no official measurement of compliance in the country's 64 universities and polytechnic schools, which process the data of more than 840,000 students. The general objective was to diagnose compliance with the LOPDP and its Regulation in the processing of students' personal data by Ecuadorian universities, through a documentary analysis of their public information and an anonymous student survey, in order to propose a risk management guide validated by experts. A mixed approach with a quantitative emphasis was adopted, with a descriptive-exploratory scope and a non-experimental cross-sectional design, without using internal data from any institution. The LOPDP was translated into thirty-one verifiable indicators: sixteen documentary indicators, applied in a census of the 64 universities with dated evidence, and fifteen perception indicators, measured with an anonymous questionnaire for adult students, with a reference sample of 384 participants and validation through expert judgment, a pilot test and Cronbach's alpha. Regulatory and perception gaps were defined, together with a six-phase guide, mapped to ISO/IEC 27005, ISO/IEC 27035-1 and NIST SP 800-61r3, and applied to fourteen sector scenarios activated by the indicators. The spreadsheet templates were verified without errors, and five of ten validation indicators meet their targets. The results of the census, the survey and the Delphi panel will be obtained between November 2026 and February 2027 and are reported as unavailable at the cut-off date.}
  {Keywords}
  {personal data protection, regulatory compliance, risk management, transparency, university students}
